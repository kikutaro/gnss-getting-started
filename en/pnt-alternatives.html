GNSS Alternatives — when GNSS is unavailable

When jamming, blockage or an outage means GNSS can't be trusted, how do you keep position and time? The gateway to "resilient PNT"

Why think about alternatives — GNSS's weaknesses

GNSS is wonderfully convenient, but not omnipotent. Because its signal is extremely weak (about −130 dBm at the ground, see the dB page), it can be knocked out in many ways. Now that even infrastructure time synchronization depends on GNSS, "what do we do without GNSS?" is an important question.

Vulnerable to jamming/spoofing

A weak signal is easily jammed or spoofed — a security weak point.

Doesn't reach indoors/underground

Inside buildings, tunnels and basements no satellites are visible.

Multipath in urban canyons

City streets add reflection errors and reduce the number of visible satellites.

Space weather / ionosphere

Solar flares and ionospheric storms degrade or interrupt accuracy.

Single point of failure

A whole society depending on one system — if it fails, the impact is wide.

The idea of PNT — GNSS is just one means

What we really want isn't "GPS" itself but PNT — Positioning, Navigation and Timing. GNSS is just one way to achieve PNT. So we flip the thinking: combine several means so it never drops out — the idea of "Resilient PNT" / "Complementary PNT".

P — Positioning

Where you are in space (latitude, longitude, height).

N — Navigation

Which way and how fast you are moving (velocity, heading).

T — Timing

Accurate time and frequency — the foundation of telecom, power and finance.

Alternatives aren't all about "completely replacing GNSS". Many act to complement it and bridge the moments it drops out (a backup). Using them together with GNSS is the norm.

🛰️

LEO-PNT (low-earth-orbit)

Positioning from satellites flying far lower than GNSS. Strong signal, jam-resistant — the leading next-generation candidate.

Inertial navigation (INS / IMU)

Integrate your own motion with accelerometers and gyros. No radio needed, fully self-contained. The classic during GNSS outages.

Terrestrial RF (eLoran, etc.)

Powerful long-wave broadcasts from ground stations. Jam-resistant, and a backup for time distribution too.

Vision / map matching (VPS)

Match "what you see" — camera, LiDAR, magnetic field, Wi-Fi — against a map. Strong indoors.

A research approach that repurposes "signals not meant for positioning" — Starlink, cellular, TV — for positioning.
