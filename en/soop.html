Signals of Opportunity (SoOP)

Repurpose signals never meant for positioning. The sky is already full of usable radio

The idea — gratefully use the radio that's already there

We're surrounded by radio not built for positioning — communications satellites like Starlink, cellular base stations, TV and radio broadcasts. Signals of Opportunity (SoOP) is the idea of repurposing them for positioning. The name means "use whatever you get the chance to".

The method resembles GNSS. Position is solved from cues like the difference in arrival times, the Doppler (frequency shift) as a source moves, and known signal patterns. LEO comms satellites in particular race across the sky, so their large Doppler is a good positioning cue.

Examples of usable "opportunity signals"

🛰️

Starlink / OneWeb (LEO comms)

Many, strong, worldwide. Studies have demonstrated metre-level positioning from the signals between traffic. The "repurposed" counterpart to purpose-built LEO-PNT.

📱

Cellular (LTE / 5G)

Positioning from base-station signals. 5G standardizes positioning, aiming for metre-level indoors. The denser the base stations in a city, the better.

📺

TV / radio broadcast

Powerful, stable terrestrial signals. Position from arrival-time differences. Strong and hard to jam.

📶

Wi-Fi

The everyday opportunity signal. Usable for indoor positioning via arrival time or signal strength (overlaps with vision/map matching).

Pros and cons

Free and already there

No need to build dedicated satellites or transmitters — use the radio already flying.

Strong and diverse

Many signals stronger than GNSS, and varied in type. Jamming them all at once is hard.

The difficulty of "not dedicated"

The signal content and timing aren't arranged for positioning. Without operator cooperation, accuracy takes ingenuity.

Still research stage

Demonstrations progress, but the "buy a receiver and anyone can use it" maturity of GNSS is yet to come.
