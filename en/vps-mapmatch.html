Vision / Map Matching (VPS / SLAM)

Fix your position by matching "what you see" to a map, not by radio. Strong indoors, underground and in cities

The idea — guess "where am I" from the surroundings

Even in an unfamiliar station, a person can tell where they are from the signs, pillars and corridor shapes. Environment-based positioning makes machines do the same. Sensors capture what is visible now and match it against a prepared map or database to find the current location. Using no radio, it comes into its own exactly where GNSS can't reach — indoors, underground, tunnels and urban canyons.

① ObserveMeasure surroundings with camera, LiDAR, magnetic field, Wi-Fi, etc.
② MatchCompare the observation against a known map / feature database
③ EstimateThe best-fitting place = current position and attitude

Main methods

📷

Visual positioning (VPS)

Match a phone camera image against a feature-point database that 3D-maps a city. AR navigation (e.g. Google's VPS) puts pedestrians at tens-of-cm to metre level.

🟣

LiDAR / SLAM

Measure the shape of surroundings with laser point clouds, building a map and estimating your position at once (SLAM). The core of self-driving cars and cleaning robots.

🧲

Magnetic fingerprint

The quirks of the magnetic field created by a building's steel differ by place. Map those fingerprints for indoor positioning — no special equipment needed.

📶

Wi-Fi / BLE positioning

Use the ID/strength pattern of Wi-Fi and beacons visible at each spot as a fingerprint. In practical use in malls and airports.

🛣️

Map matching

A car nav snaps position to the road network. The classic method of absorbing error with the map constraint that "the car must be on a road".

🌡️

Barometric altimeter

Estimate height — i.e. which floor — from air pressure. Combined with horizontal positioning for 3D indoor location.

Pros and cons

Strong indoors/cities

Its home turf is exactly where GNSS is weakest — underground malls included.

Hard to spoof

Faking the "scenery" or magnetic field is difficult. Unrelated to RF jamming.

Needs a prior map

The reference DB (3D map, fingerprint map) must be built and maintained.

Sensitive to change

Renovation, construction, darkness or bad weather change the appearance and drop accuracy. Compute cost is high too.

In practice you switch seamlessly — GNSS outdoors, VPS or magnetic indoors — and bridge the gaps with inertial navigation (INS). Big picture: GNSS Alternatives (overview).
