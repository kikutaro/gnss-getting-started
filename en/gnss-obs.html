WHY "PSEUDO"? — THE RECEIVER CLOCK ERROR HITS EVERY SATELLITE EQUALLY

Outline = true range / fill = measured pseudorange. Move the clock error δt and every satellite's measurement shifts by the same amount.

A mere 0.1 ms clock offset stretches or shrinks ALL the ranges by ~30 km. Because the offset is common to every satellite, it can be solved as a 4th unknown together with position — the core idea of GNSS, and why you need at least 4 satellites.

Inside the pseudorange — the error budget

The measured pseudorange ρ is the true geometric range r plus several errors:

ρ = r + c(δt_receiver − δt_satellite) + ionosphere + troposphere + multipath + noise

Error sourceTypical sizeRemedy

The satellite clock is fixed by the message; the receiver clock is solved away. The remaining fight against ionosphere and multipath is the history of high-precision GNSS.

From chip resolution to carrier phase — the road to cm

As the experiment shows, missing the correlation peak by one chip means ~293 m (L5: ~29 m). Real receivers interpolate the peak shape to ~1/100 chip, so code pseudoranges resolve to tens of cm to a few m.

The next level is carrier-phase positioning: the L1 carrier wavelength is just 19 cm, and reading its phase to 1/100 gives mm resolution. But the carrier repeats identically, so "which wavelength number" (the integer ambiguity) is unknown — resolving it is what RTK and PPP do, achieving cm-level fixes. QZSS's CLAS (L6) broadcasts data that helps resolve it.

How GNSS uses Doppler

GPS satellites move at ~3.9 km/s, producing up to about ±5 kHz of Doppler on L1 even for a stationary receiver (this page's flat-earth geometry exaggerates a little). The shift scales with carrier frequency — L5 shows ~75% of L1's shift. Try switching bands.

For a receiver, Doppler is:

(1) An acquisition hurdle: finding a signal needs a 2-D search over code phase × Doppler frequency. A-GNSS (rough position and time from the network) shrinks that search dramatically.
(2) A velocity sensor: receiver velocity comes straight from Doppler at cm/s level. Car-nav speed and heading come from Doppler, not position differences.
(3) The tracking reference: the PLL keeps chasing the ever-changing Doppler, which is what makes carrier-phase measurement possible at all.

Historically, the pre-GPS system "Transit" (1960s) positioned ships from the shape of the Doppler curve alone. The S-curve crosses zero exactly at closest approach — its shape encodes the geometry between you and the orbit.
