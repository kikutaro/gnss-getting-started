Finding the gain "sweet spot" — between too weak and too strong

An SDR spectrum is not the raw radio itself but the view "after the front-end and ADC". There are two walls here.

The lower wall (noise floor): at low gain the ADC's quantization noise is high when referred to the input, and weak signals sink below it. Raise the gain and the quantization noise drops until you reach the true floor set by the front-end thermal noise (NF) — the limit for seeing weak signals.

The upper wall (ADC full scale): too much gain and strong components exceed the ADC's ceiling, clipping (saturating), flattening the waveform and raising false spurs in the spectrum. Lower the gain or add attenuation.

So the answer is: raise the gain as high as you can without clipping, and bring the floor all the way down to thermal noise. Move the gain in the simulator above to get a feel for tucking the GPS trapezoid between the noise floor (purple) and the full-scale line (pink).

RBW (sample rate) changes what you see

The noise in each spectrum bin scales with its width (resolution bandwidth, RBW): noise = −174 dBm/Hz + NF + 10log₁₀(RBW). Narrower RBW (lower sample rate / more FFT points) lowers the per-bin noise, so weak signals emerge more easily.

But GPS is a spread signal, so even at narrow RBW it never becomes a sharp peak — the floor just rises slightly into a gentle trapezoid. Seeing a real satellite (~−130 dBm) raw on a single SDR sweep is still hard; to see it clearly you correlate (despread) in a receiver to gain the processing gain (see the Spectrum page and Pseudorange).

The actual procedure (with LimeSuite / GQRX, etc.)

① Set the center frequency to 1575.42 MHz and the sample rate to a few Msps (e.g. 2.5–10). ② Raise the RX gain from low, stopping just before the noise floor starts to lift (about when you reach the thermal floor). ③ Connect the source (gps-sdr-sim + an SDR TX, or a signal generator) by cable and attenuate to around −50 dB into the input. ④ If the signal is so strong it clips (many spurs, the whole floor rises), back off with gain or ATT.

To go all the way to a position fix, after checking the spectrum feed the raw I/Q into GNSS-SDR / PocketSDR for correlation, tracking and decoding — the blocks from the Receive Chain actually run.
