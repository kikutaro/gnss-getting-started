What is phase (φ)?

Phase says "where in its cycle the wave is right now", expressed as an angle. One cycle = 360° (2π radians). Increasing φ shifts the waveform to the left (earlier in time). Phase turns "same shape, different timing" into a number.

Look at the rotating phasor on the right. A wave is the vertical shadow of an arrow rotating at constant speed. The arrow's angle is 2πft + φ, and φ is the starting angle (initial phase). Phase modulation of radio waves (PSK) puts information on exactly this angle.

Superposition and interference — the "Antiphase" preset

When several waves share the same space, the value at each instant is a simple sum (superposition). At the same frequency, a 0° phase difference reinforces (double amplitude), while 180° (antiphase) cancels completely to zero. Noise-cancelling earphones work on this principle.

Pick the "Antiphase" preset and nudge Wave B's phase away from 180° — watch the cancellation fall apart.

Beats — adding waves of slightly different frequency

Add two waves of slightly different frequency (e.g. 1.0 Hz and 1.2 Hz) and the sum's amplitude slowly swells and fades. That is a "beat", and its rate equals the frequency difference |fB − fA| (here 0.2 Hz → a 5-second cycle). Beats underlie instrument tuning and radio frequency mixers.

Harmonics — building new shapes from sines

The "3rd harmonic" preset adds a wave at 3× the frequency and 1/3 the amplitude to a 1 Hz fundamental. The sum starts to look square-ish. In fact, summing infinitely many sines can build any periodic waveform — square, triangle, anything (Fourier series). Put the other way: any signal can be decomposed into sines — the single most important idea in radio and signal processing.
