About the "antenna + GNSS module + PC" split

Commercial receivers (u-blox modules, the chip in your phone) pack everything from the LNA to the protocol output into one chip or module. With an active antenna, the LNA lives in the antenna itself, so the signal survives a long coax run to the module.

The PC normally just consumes the finished position — but software like RTKLIB can take raw observables (pseudorange, carrier phase, Doppler) and compute high-precision solutions on the PC side. u-blox's UBX-RXM-RAWX messages are exactly that raw data.

Where corrections act (standalone L1 C/A)

Even "standalone" positioning is full of corrections: satellite clock error via the nav message's af0/af1/af2, ionosphere via the broadcast Klobuchar model (only ~half removed), troposphere via a built-in model. All are applied right before PVT, when the pseudoranges are being cleaned up.

External corrections add on top: QZSS L1S (sub-meter, Japan area), SBAS, or network RTK (cm-level) — all injected into the same corrections stage on capable receivers. QZSS's L1S cleverly arrives on the same frequency and modulation as L1 C/A, so the antenna and RF front-end need no changes.
