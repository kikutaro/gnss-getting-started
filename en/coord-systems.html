Coordinate Frames — LLH · ECEF · ENU · RTN

The frameworks that turn "where" into numbers. The main frames used in GNSS and LEO, and how to pick and convert between them

A frame is a choice of origin and axes

The same place gets different numbers depending on where you put the origin and which way the axes point. GNSS uses different frames for different jobs and converts between them as needed. Four are enough to know.

LLH geodetic

Latitude, longitude, height. The human-facing form maps and GNSS output use. Referenced to an ellipsoid (WGS84).

ECEF Earth-centered

X/Y/Z Cartesian with the origin at Earth's center. The basis of GNSS computation; rotates with the Earth.

ENU local

Origin at a point on the surface: East, North, Up. Handles azimuth/elevation and "how far east".

RTN orbital

Origin at a satellite: radial, along-track, orbit-normal. For orbit errors and relative motion.

① LLH geodetic — latitude, longitude, ellipsoidal height

aka =geodetic coordinateslat/lon/height(φ, λ, h)BLH
φ lat angle the ellipsoid normal makes with the equatorial plane (≠ geocentric latitude)
λ lon east–west angle from the prime meridian
h height height above the ellipsoid (= ellipsoidal height)

The human-facing coordinate GNSS ultimately outputs and maps use. It is referenced to the reference ellipsoid WGS84 (semi-major axis a=6,378,137 m, flattening 1/f=298.257223563). Watch the height: LLH's h is ellipsoidal height, which differs from the "elevation (above mean sea level = geoid)" we usually mean (→ Coordinates & Height Basics).

② ECEF — Earth-Centered, Earth-Fixed Cartesian (WGS84)

aka =Earth-Centered Earth-FixedECEF (X, Y, Z)ECR
X toward lat 0, lon 0 (equator × prime meridian)
Z toward the spin axis (north pole)
Y completes the right-handed set (lon 90°E)

The origin is Earth's center of mass. Because it rotates with the Earth, a building on the ground always has the same X/Y/Z. GNSS computes both satellite positions and the receiver solution in ECEF first, then converts to LLH for display.

LLH→ (closed form) →ECEF → (iterate, e.g. Bowring) →LLH

LLH→ECEF is a closed-form formula; ECEF→LLH is solved by iteration (or an approximation). Note: ECI (Earth-Centered Inertial, non-rotating) is different — orbits are computed in ECI and converted to ECEF using time and Earth's rotation angle.

③ ENU — local frame with the origin at a surface point

aka / family =local tangent planeEast-North-UpNED (North-East-Down)NEU (North-East-Up)
E East
N North
U Up (= local vertical / ellipsoid normal)

A frame with its origin at a reference point on the surface and axes along the local east, north and up. Intuitive for people, it is used for a satellite's azimuth and elevation, for "so many metres east and north of the reference", and for expressing local errors. Rotating an ECEF vector by a rotation matrix set by the reference point's latitude/longitude gives ENU. Fields order the axes differently: aerospace and robotics use NED (north-east-down), and the NEU (north-east-up) you asked about is the same local frame reordered.

④ RTN — orbital frame with the origin at a satellite (big in LEO)

aka / near-synonyms =Radial-Tangential-NormalRSWRIC (Radial-In track-Cross track)LVLH
R Radial = from Earth's center toward the satellite
T Tangential / In-track = along the orbit (≈ velocity)
N Normal / Cross-track = perpendicular to the orbit plane (angular-momentum direction)

A frame glued to the satellite. From the position vector r and velocity v it is built as R=r/|r|, N=(r×v)/|r×v|, T=N×R. It is ideal for splitting orbit errors and satellite-to-satellite relative motion into radial, along-track and out-of-plane, and is widely used in LEO-PNT, precise orbit determination and formation flying.

Aside: what mostly affects ranging (pseudorange) is the radial (R) orbit error; along-track (T) and cross-track (N) errors matter less geometrically — splitting error in RTN shows "which component hurts positioning". RSW and RIC are near-synonyms (with slightly different axis conventions).

The conversion map

LLH lat/lon/height ⇄ ECEF Earth-center ⇄ ENU local (rotate by the point's φ, λ)
ECI inertial ⇄ ECEF (time · Earth rotation angle) / orbit r,v → RTN satellite

Inside GNSS, satellite orbits are solved in inertial ECI → ECEF, the receiver solution is found in ECEF → displayed in LLH, and user-facing azimuth/elevation is in ENU — hopping across frames all the way.
