GNSS Study

Interactive lessons from wave basics to the inside of a GNSS receiver. Follow the menu on the left (☰ on mobile) from top to bottom — that's the recommended learning route.

STEP 0What is GNSS — start with the big picture

  1. 01 What is GNSS?

    Start from "I know GPS, but what's GNSS?" — how the world's systems (GPS, Galileo, BeiDou, QZSS…) relate, how positioning works, and everyday uses, all in animated figures.

STEP 1Basics — building your radio & positioning toolbox

  1. 02 Wave Basics

    y = A sin(2πft + φ). Move amplitude, frequency and phase with sliders; watch superposition, beats and harmonics. Where everything starts.

  2. 03 Time & Clocks

    1 µs = 300 m. Feel the world where clock error is distance error — clock drift race, relativity in GPS. GNSS is a system of clocks.

  3. 04 dB & Signal Power

    How weak is −130 dBm? A dB converter and a link budget show how a signal below the noise floor gets received.

  4. 05 Modulation Basics (AM/FM/PSK)

    Where does information ride the wave? Compare the three schemes in animation, and see why GNSS chose BPSK.

  5. 06 Coordinates & Height Basics

    Ellipsoid, geoid and elevation (why GPS altitude disagrees with maps), what latitude really is, and ECEF — get ready to read positions correctly.

STEP 2How GNSS works — from satellite to PC

  1. 07 GNSS Signal Simulator

    Satellite radio raining from the sky (GPS + QZSS). Play with satellite count, building blockage, multipath and the L1/L2/L5/L6 bands.

  2. 08 Frequency Band Map

    Every GNSS's bands on one frequency axis — shared frequencies and system-specific bands at a glance.

  3. 09 L1 C/A Signal Structure

    Carrier × PRN code × navigation message. A 3-level waveform zoom and block diagram explain spreading and BPSK.

  4. 10 GPS on a Spectrum Analyzer

    What GPS looks like on a spectrum analyzer: a sinc² trapezoid sinking into the noise, C/A over P(Y), and how it differs from an FM station.

  5. 11 Navigation Messages

    LNAV/CNAV/CNAV-2 bit by bit — clock, ephemeris and almanac, with GPS and QZSS side by side.

  6. 12 Pseudorange & Doppler

    Find the delay yourself with code correlation, see how clock bias makes ranges "pseudo", and watch the Doppler S-curve.

  7. 13 Time Sync

    Why clocks keep coming up in positioning: the rubidium/caesium atomic clocks aboard satellites, a clock-drift comparison, GPS time vs UTC and leap seconds, and use cases for "distributing time" — mobile base stations, power grids, finance and more.

  8. 14 Errors & Corrections

    Erase satellite-clock, ionosphere and troposphere errors with broadcast, dual-frequency, SBAS and RTK/PPP/CLAS toggles. Feel the correction take you from metres to centimetres.

  9. 15 Receive Chain — Satellite to PC

    Antenna, LNA, inside the GNSS module (acquisition, tracking, decoding, corrections, PVT) to NMEA output — the grand tour.

  10. 16 QZSS Michibiki

    An animated figure-8 orbit shows how it loiters above Japan. Its own services — signal authentication, disaster reports, CLAS — and its place alongside NavIC as a regional GNSS.

STEP 3SDR in practice — handling GNSS signals yourself

  1. 17 SDR Basics & Boards

    What software-defined radio is, plus the classic boards — USRP, bladeRF, ADALM-Pluto, LimeSDR, HackRF One — on a frequency-coverage chart and comparison table.

  2. 18 SDR & Spectrum

    How GPS L1 (1575.42 MHz) looks when received on a LimeSDR XTRX (LMS7002M). Vary the source, attenuator and RX gain to feel how the signal fits between the noise floor and ADC full scale.

  3. 19 Transmitting GNSS with SDR

    Generate GPS/QZSS L1 C/A with LimeSDR XTRX / Mini 2.0. Design the attenuation that lands at the real-satellite −130 dBm, and learn the legal cautions.

STEP 4Threats & defence — how GNSS gets fooled

  1. 20 Jamming, Spoofing & Meaconing

    Watch what each attack does to a receiver in the simulator, learn to diagnose them from C/N₀, AGC and position symptoms, and see the defences — multi-frequency, CRPA antennas, signal authentication.

STEP 5GNSS alternatives — PNT when GNSS is unavailable

  1. 21 GNSS Alternatives (overview)

    Why alternatives are needed (GNSS's weaknesses), the idea of PNT (positioning, navigation, timing), and a catalogue and comparison of the options. The gateway to resilient PNT.

  2. 22 LEO-PNT (low-earth-orbit)

    Positioning from satellites flying far lower than GNSS. A simulator to see how signal strength changes with altitude, plus efforts like Xona and Iridium STL.

  3. 23 Inertial navigation (INS / IMU)

    Self-contained positioning with accelerometers and gyros, no radio. A simulator of how drift grows after GNSS is lost, through to GNSS/INS fusion.

  4. 24 Terrestrial RF (eLoran, etc.)

    Powerful long-wave broadcasts from ground stations. A jam-resistant, independent alternative that also backs up time distribution.

  5. 25 Vision / map matching (VPS)

    Match "what you see" — camera, LiDAR, magnetic field, Wi-Fi — against a map. Strong exactly where GNSS struggles: indoors and in cities.

  6. 26 Signals of Opportunity (SoOP)

    Repurposing signals not meant for positioning — Starlink, cellular, TV — as a positioning source. How it differs from LEO-PNT.

STEP 6Tools — to get hands-on

  1. 27 GNSS Open-Source Catalog

    GNSS-SDR, PocketSDR, RTKLIB, gps-sdr-sim and more — the classic receive / positioning / signal-generation tools, grouped by purpose so you can see at a glance what each does.
