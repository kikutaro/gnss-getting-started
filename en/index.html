GNSS Getting Started

Interactive lessons from wave basics to the inside of a GNSS receiver. Follow the menu on the left (☰ on mobile) from top to bottom — that's the recommended learning route.

STEP 1Basics — building your radio & positioning toolbox

  1. 01 Wave Basics

    y = A sin(2πft + φ). Move amplitude, frequency and phase with sliders; watch superposition, beats and harmonics. Where everything starts.

  2. 02 Time & Clocks

    1 µs = 300 m. Feel the world where clock error is distance error — clock drift race, relativity in GPS. GNSS is a system of clocks.

  3. 03 dB & Signal Power

    How weak is −130 dBm? A dB converter and a link budget show how a signal below the noise floor gets received.

  4. 04 Modulation Basics (AM/FM/PSK)

    Where does information ride the wave? Compare the three schemes in animation, and see why GNSS chose BPSK.

  5. 05 Coordinates & Height Basics

    Ellipsoid, geoid and elevation (why GPS altitude disagrees with maps), what latitude really is, and ECEF — get ready to read positions correctly.

STEP 2How GNSS works — from satellite to PC

  1. 06 GNSS Signal Simulator

    Satellite radio raining from the sky (GPS + QZSS). Play with satellite count, building blockage, multipath and the L1/L2/L5/L6 bands.

  2. 07 Frequency Band Map

    Every GNSS's bands on one frequency axis — shared frequencies and system-specific bands at a glance.

  3. 08 L1 C/A Signal Structure

    Carrier × PRN code × navigation message. A 3-level waveform zoom and block diagram explain spreading and BPSK.

  4. 09 Navigation Messages

    LNAV/CNAV/CNAV-2 bit by bit — clock, ephemeris and almanac, with GPS and QZSS side by side.

  5. 10 Pseudorange & Doppler

    Find the delay yourself with code correlation, see how clock bias makes ranges "pseudo", and watch the Doppler S-curve.

  6. 11 Receive Chain — Satellite to PC

    Antenna, LNA, inside the GNSS module (acquisition, tracking, decoding, corrections, PVT) to NMEA output — the grand tour.
